About Me

Zamal Haider Shish is an aspiring instructional designer and e-learning developer with professional experience in ERP implementation, software training, and technology based learning.

He began his professional career in 2018 and later moved into ERP implementation, where he worked with clients and trained end-users on complex software processes in real workplace environments.

Zamal is completing an MS in Instructional Design and Technology at the University of Tampa, and holds a bachelor's in Software Engineering with Multimedia from Limkokwing University in Malaysia. His interests include e-learning development, Articulate Rise and Storyline, multimedia learning, and AI in education.

He has worked on ERP implementation projects and more recently completed an instructional design internship where he redesigned the retail management course using Articulate Rise and created interactive learning experiences. Zamal is currently developing instructional design and e-learning projects that combine technology and learner-centered design. He lives in Plant City, Florida.

Professional Goal and Self-Assessment

My goal is to begin my career as an instructional designer, building on the M.S. in Instructional Design and Technology program at the University of Tampa and the professional experience I bring from ERP software implementation and corporate training.

Instructional design work calls for a mix of skills: analyzing learners and content, writing clear objectives, structuring instruction logically, building interactive eLearning in tools such as Articulate Rise 360 and Storyline 360, and revising a course based on feedback. I am still early in formally practicing several of these skills, and I want this self-assessment to be honest rather than inflated.

My strongest foundation is working directly with end users and translating complex processes into something they can learn and apply, built over several years of ERP implementation and software training where I regularly explained unfamiliar systems to people under real workplace pressure. My instructional design internship, where I redesigned a Retail Management course in Articulate Rise 360, gave me my first hands-on experience applying a formal design process rather than informal, on-the-job training. My coursework and the eLearning projects in this portfolio are where I am continuing to build fluency with Rise 360, Storyline 360, scenario-based design, and evaluation methods — areas I am actively developing, not areas I have mastered.

The sections below connect the instructional design skills I am developing to the specific portfolio projects and experience that demonstrate them.

Instructional Design and Course Development

Evidence: Retail Management Course Redesign → view case study

During my instructional design internship with Braathe Enterprises, I supported the redesign of an existing Retail Management course, working through course analysis, design, implementation, evaluation, and revision, and incorporating feedback into iterative improvements. This project is my clearest evidence of applying a structured design process and building in assessment through knowledge checks, rather than starting from an informal or ad hoc approach.

eLearning Development with Articulate Rise 360 and Storyline 360

Evidence: Disaster Response Simulator (Storyline 360) · Retail Management Course Redesign · Professional Email Communication for Workplace Success (both Rise 360)

I have built eLearning in both of Articulate's major authoring tools. The Retail Management Course Redesign and Professional Email Communication for Workplace Success were developed in Rise 360, and the Disaster Response Simulator was built in Storyline 360 to support branching, scenario-based interaction. Working across both tools has given me a practical sense of when a simpler, responsive Rise course fits the content and when Storyline's more flexible interactions are needed.

Interactive and Scenario-Based Learning

Evidence: Disaster Response Simulator → view case study

The Disaster Response Simulator is an interactive hurricane and disaster-response learning experience built around scenarios and decision-making activities rather than static content. Designing it meant thinking through branching choices and consequences, which is the skill I most want to keep building as I take on more complex scenario-based work.

Multimedia and Learner-Centered Design

Evidence: Retail Management Course Redesign · The Science Behind Nutrition

As part of the Retail Management Course Redesign, I developed multimedia learning resources and knowledge checks to support the redesigned content. Alongside it, The Science Behind Nutrition, also built in Rise 360, reflects my ongoing practice translating subject matter into a structured, self-paced digital course organized around how learners move through content.

Evaluation, Feedback, and Continuous Improvement

Evidence: Retail Management Course Redesign → view case study

My internship work on the Retail Management course followed a full evaluation and revision cycle — analysis, implementation, and evaluation — and I incorporated feedback into iterative improvements rather than treating the first draft as final. That is the habit I most want to carry into future projects: testing a design with real users or reviewers, not just completing a build.

Technology Integration and Stakeholder Collaboration

Evidence: ERP implementation experience, Pakiza Software Ltd. and Logic Software Ltd. → view experience

Before instructional design, I spent several years in ERP software implementation, working directly with clients to understand business requirements, training end users on new systems, and evaluating whether a client's existing processes matched standard practice. That work required ongoing collaboration with clients and end users across each implementation, a dynamic I expect to meet again when designing training for people inside an organization. I am carrying that stakeholder-facing experience into instructional design work, including the internship, where I worked within an organization's existing course and feedback process.

A sixth project in this portfolio, Leading with Emotional Intelligence, is still being documented. Once its content, development tool, and design details are confirmed, it will be added to the skills evidence above.

Looking Ahead

This portfolio will keep developing alongside my coursework. I expect to publish interactive Rise 360 and Storyline 360 demos directly on the site, finish documenting Leading with Emotional Intelligence, and add new projects as I complete them. Together, the experience and projects described above are the evidence I currently have for an entry-level instructional design role — not a finished skill set, but a foundation I intend to keep building through the rest of this program and into my first position in the field.
